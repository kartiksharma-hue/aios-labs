The most expensive mistake in paid media is sending campaign traffic to a homepage. A homepage serves ten audiences at once; a campaign visitor arrived with one specific expectation set by one specific ad. When the page does not repeat that promise in the first screen, the visitor leaves and you pay for it anyway.

We build pages that continue the ad rather than restart the conversation (same promise, same visual language, same offer) then strip out every element that does not serve the conversion.

Scope

What landing pages includes

Message match to the ad

The headline continues the ad's promise word-for-word where it should. Visual continuity from creative to page is one of the cheapest conversion gains available.

Above-the-fold clarity

What it is, who it is for, what it costs or what happens next, and one obvious action. All visible without scrolling on a phone.

Objection handling in sequence

The page answers doubts in the order they occur: does this work, does it work for someone like me, what does it cost, what if it goes wrong, how do I start.

Proof placement

Results, screenshots, testimonials and logos positioned next to the claims they support rather than dumped in one block halfway down.

Friction removal

Form fields cut to what sales genuinely needs, mobile keyboard types set correctly, inline validation, and a submit path with no surprises.

Instrumentation

Scroll depth, section visibility, form field drop-off and CTA clicks tracked so the next iteration is based on behaviour, not opinion.

    Variant testing on headline, offer framing, proof and form length, based on where the recorded drop-off actually is.

Answers

Landing Pages: frequently asked

What makes a good landing page?

One goal, a headline that matches the ad that brought the visitor, immediate clarity on what is being offered and to whom, proof placed beside the claims, objections answered in the order they arise, minimal form friction, and fast loading on mobile. Almost every underperforming page fails at least three of those.

Should paid traffic go to a landing page or the homepage?

A landing page, in nearly every case. A homepage is built for many audiences and many intents; a campaign visitor arrives with exactly one. The exception is a strong brand-name search where the visitor genuinely wants the full site.

How many form fields should we have?

As few as sales can work with. Every additional field costs completions. If qualification is essential, ask the qualifying questions after the contact details are captured, or on the thank-you page, so a partial answer still leaves you a contactable lead.

How long does a landing page take?

Typically five to ten working days from brief to live, including copy, design, build and tracking. Variants of an existing page are faster, usually two to three days.

Do you A/B test the pages?

Yes, once there is enough traffic for a result to mean anything. Below roughly a hundred conversions per variant, most tests are noise, so early on we focus on structural fixes with obvious logic behind them rather than statistical testing.

Can you build the page on our existing website?

Yes: as a page on your Shopify, WordPress or custom site, or as a standalone fast-loading page on a subdomain or path. Keeping it on the main domain is usually better for tracking consistency and for any SEO value the page picks up.
